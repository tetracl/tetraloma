# M18: Mesh-independent device and communication crates

Design: docs/design.md sections 7, 9, 11, 12, 14, 16, 18, 22 and 29; also 23 and 25 for the protected paths and the standing matrix; ADR-0074
Depends on: none
Milestone issue: created with the task issues, after this plan is merged

## Scope

M18 carries out ADR-0074. It changes where items live and which error type a crate returns. It changes no kernel, no policy and no numerical result.

- **tetraloma-comm** (new; a published library crate, host-only): the communicators and what belongs to them move out of tetraloma-parallel: `CommDyn`, `Comm`, `CommExt`, `DType`, `CommScalar`, `Serial`, `Mpi`, `Neighbourhood`, `owner_hash`, `local_gpu_index` and the `mpi::ffi` wrappers. `ReduceOp` moves there from tetraloma-device. The crate gets `CommError` and its `Result`.
- **tetraloma-device:** `DeviceError` and its `Result`; `FieldScalar` beside `Real`; `ReduceOp` re-exported. `GridError`, its `Result` and `ConnKey` leave the crate.
- **tetraloma-parallel:** keeps `IndexMap` and `Scatterer`, gets `ParallelError` and its `Result`, and re-exports the public items of tetraloma-comm except `Result`.
- **tetraloma-partition:** depends on tetraloma-comm instead of tetraloma-parallel, so it is free of CubeCL, and returns `PartitionError`.
- **tetraloma-topology:** the new home of `GridError`, its `Result` and `ConnKey`, with the `From` conversions from the three foundation errors. tetraloma re-exports them and converts `PartitionError` with a helper.
- **Test support below the mesh:** `tetraloma-comm-testing` (host-only) and `tetraloma-testing-base` (device), both unpublished. tetraloma-testing re-exports them, so every existing import holds.
- **A foundation-only test crate** (unpublished; no name in the design yet, question 1): primitives and the scatterer tested without a mesh crate in the build.
- **Checks:** dependency rule 9 and the two `--no-cubecl` checks in `scripts/check-workspace-deps.sh`; a new check of the public names of the four foundation crates.
- **User documentation:** tetraloma-comm joins the audited crates and the site; the book follows each move.

Unchanged for users of `tetraloma`: every path that `tetraloma` and `tetraloma::prelude` export, every `GridError` variant, every numerical result.

What does change for them: the foundation items that `tetraloma` re-exports return their own crate's error. `IndexMap` and `Scatterer` return `ParallelError`, the communicators `CommError`, the methods of `DeviceBuffer` `DeviceError`, and `Partitioner::partition` `PartitionError`. In a function that returns `tetraloma::Result`, `?` converts the first three. A function that passes such a result on without `?`, and every implementation of `Partitioner` or `CommDyn`, must change. The book has one of each (`tutorials/halo-exchange.md`, and `RoundRobin` in `tetraloma-examples/src/distributed_mesh.rs`); question 3 is about the names such code needs.

Left out:

- An open set of kernel families: `KernelKind` keeps its 13 variants and `VectorPolicy::quad_tile` stays (the roadmap entry). Scatter begin and end, point-to-point messages, communicator splitting and the GPU-aware transport stay out too.
- The existing multi-rank tests of `IndexMap`, `Scatterer` and `Partitioner` in `tetraloma/tests/` (`index_map_threads.rs`, `scatter_threads.rs`, `partition_threads.rs`) stay where they are. They import through `tetraloma`, so they show that the facade's paths still hold. `partition_threads.rs` builds its graphs from test meshes and could not move below the mesh.
- The `#[doc(hidden)] pub` exposure of `Strategy` and the degree-policy reader of tetraloma-geometry is M14's: tetraloma-function, which reads them, does not exist yet. They already live in tetraloma-geometry, so M18 has nothing to move.
- `UnsupportedElement` and `SpaceMismatch` (Listing F1) come with M14, in `GridError`'s new home.

M18 adds and changes no kernel. So there is no vector-size invariance test, no task has a remote run, and CUDA first sees M18's changes at the gate. The roadmap entry names no runs.

### Where the code differs from the design today

ADR-0074 edited the design ahead of the code, so the differences below are the milestone's work. Read from the code on the main branch at abb4d70.

1. **No tetraloma-comm.** The communicators are seven files of tetraloma-parallel (`comm.rs`, `counts.rs`, `graph.rs`, `hash.rs`, `local_gpu.rs`, `mpi.rs`, `serial.rs`; 1,924 lines). None of them uses a device type or CubeCL; from tetraloma-device they take only `GridError`, `Result` and `ReduceOp`. They return `GridError` (47 lines name it).
2. **`ReduceOp`** is defined in `tetraloma-device/src/primitives/mod.rs`.
3. **`GridError`** (31 variants), its `Result` and `ConnKey` are in `tetraloma-device/src/error.rs`. tetraloma-parallel re-exports `GridError`, `ReduceOp` and `Result` from tetraloma-device; tetraloma re-exports `GridError`, `Result` and `ConnKey` from tetraloma-device too.
4. **No foundation error types.** `DeviceError`, `CommError`, `ParallelError` and `PartitionError` do not exist. `GridError` is named on 174 lines of tetraloma-device, 37 of `index_map.rs` and `scatterer.rs`, and 92 of tetraloma-partition.
5. **tetraloma-partition** depends on tetraloma-parallel and imports `CommDyn`, `CommExt`, `Serial`, `GridError` and `Result` from it. Its dev-dependency tetraloma-testing is not used by any of its code.
6. **tetraloma-io** lists tetraloma-partition as a dependency, and no source file uses it.
7. **Imports above the foundation.** 148 lines of tetraloma-topology, tetraloma-geometry, tetraloma-io, tetraloma and tetraloma-bench name `GridError`, `Result` or `ConnKey` through `tetraloma_device::` or `tetraloma_parallel::`, and about 30 more inside multi-line imports.
8. **Two crate-private items cross the new crate boundary.**
   - `agree` (`comm.rs`) is called only by `index_map.rs`. It stays in tetraloma-parallel. Its unit test passes it a `GridError::DuplicateOriginId`, a variant `ParallelError` will not have.
   - `Neighbourhood::comm()` (`comm.rs`) is called by `scatterer.rs`. Listing S2c gives `Neighbourhood` no accessor for the communicator it holds.
9. **Test support** is all in tetraloma-testing, which depends on tetraloma: `ThreadComm` (`comm.rs`, 612 lines, importing through `tetraloma`), `comm_checks.rs` (350 lines: the communicator checks, the `IndexMap` checks and the ghost layouts in one module), `world_size.rs` (the rank-count guard), and the client functions and tolerances in `lib.rs`. `ThreadComm`'s rank budget asks tetraloma-testing's backend parser whether the test device is the CPU runtime.
10. **Dev-dependencies through the facade.** tetraloma-device and tetraloma-parallel dev-depend on tetraloma-testing: 97 lines of tetraloma-device's examples and tests and 9 lines of tetraloma-parallel's examples name `tetraloma_testing::`. The doctest runs of `scripts/check-docs.sh`, `scripts/check-user-docs.sh`, `ci.yml` and `ptscotch.yml` pass `tetraloma-testing/mpi` as a dependency feature of those crates.
11. **`FieldScalar`** does not exist, and `num-complex` is not among the workspace dependencies.
12. **The documentation audit** lists nine crates in `scripts/doc_audit.py`, `scripts/check-user-docs.sh` and the `docs` job of `ci.yml`; `scripts/build-site.sh` takes its list from `doc_audit.py`. M11's task T4 (#72, open) adds tetraloma-element to the same lists. Whichever of the two tasks lands second merges the lists; the counts in comments follow the lists.
13. **`scripts/check-workspace-deps.sh`** takes one `--no-cubecl` crate and has no check of rule 9.
14. **The foundation-only test crate** of the roadmap entry has no name and no row in the crate table of section 18 (question 1).
15. **MPI tests** live in tetraloma-mpi-tests, which depends on tetraloma (section 22, "MPI tests"). The roadmap entry asks for "the same scatter tests" of the foundation-only crate under MPICH and Open MPI (question 2).
16. **The facade does not export the new error types.** Section 18 has tetraloma re-export `GridError`, `Result` and `ConnKey` and convert `PartitionError`; it lists no re-export of `CommError`, `DeviceError`, `ParallelError` or `PartitionError`. tetraloma-examples and tetraloma-testing implement `Partitioner` and `CommDyn` with the names they get from `tetraloma`, and depend on no foundation crate (question 3).

Carried over from earlier milestones, and still true:

- Enum variants cannot be stubbed, so a T0 declares them whole (M6, T0).
- The checks shared by the thread-rank tests and the MPI targets are library functions of a test-support crate; the MPI target only starts MPI and calls them (M6, T0 and T3).
- `ThreadComm` implements `CommDyn::raw`, which exists only under `mpi`. A crate that holds `ThreadComm` needs an `mpi` feature that is on whenever the communicator crate's is (M6, T0).
- tetraloma has its own crate-private `pipeline::agree` for mesh errors (M6, T9). M18 leaves it alone.
- `IndexMap::send_lists` and `recv_lists` are hidden test accessors (M6, T5; ADR-0028). They stay in tetraloma-parallel.

### Hidden items (dependency rule 7)

M18 adds one permanent hidden item:

- `Neighbourhood::comm(&self) -> Option<&Rc<dyn CommDyn>>` in tetraloma-comm becomes `#[doc(hidden)] pub`, for `Scatterer` in tetraloma-parallel, which exchanges through the communicator its `Neighbourhood` holds (section 11). It is documented as not part of the stable API. Its named test is `neighbourhood_holds_its_parent_communicator`, a unit test of tetraloma-comm (T2).

The stubs of T0 are hidden until the task that implements them removes the attribute. No stub remains hidden after T7.

How the rows are read:

- **A1** is a shell check with a negative control, like M11's A13. `--no-mesh CRATE` adds a crate to those whose normal and build dependencies must hold no mesh crate of rule 9. tetraloma-topology depends on tetraloma-dims, so the run must fail. The default run checks the four foundation crates.
- **A2.** `--no-cubecl` becomes repeatable. From T3 on, the default run also checks both crates, over normal, build and dev dependencies as it does for tetraloma-element.
- **A3.** The check builds the API pages of the four crates and reads the names of their structs, enums, unions, traits, type aliases, functions, methods, constants and statics. It compares without regard to case, so `conn_key` or `MESH_PAD` would fail too. Enum variants are not checked (ADR-0074: `KernelKind` keeps `DofTransform`). Hidden items are not on the pages. At T0 the check fails on `GridError` and `ConnKey`, which are the only two matches today. The negative control is `scripts/check-foundation-names.sh --crates tetraloma-topology`, which must exit with status 3. `scripts/check-user-docs.sh` runs the same check on the site's pages, which are built with the site's features.
- **A4.** The test builds each of the 25 variants (5 of `CommError`, 5 of `DeviceError`, 8 of `ParallelError`, 7 of `PartitionError`) with distinct field values, converts it, and matches the `GridError` variant and its fields. The four enums are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a test outside their crates cannot prove that its list is complete. Each defining crate therefore has a unit test with a match over its own enum without a wildcard arm: a new variant breaks that crate's build and points to this test. The test is a unit test of tetraloma because the conversion helper is crate-private.
- **A5.** "Before the milestone" is the main branch at abb4d70, where the file's blob is `1663327c`. No task may touch the file, so the command passes from T0 on; every task and the gate run it. The existing check of the protected path (`cargo test -p tetraloma-device --test integration policy::`) runs as before.
- **A6, A7.** The dependency clause of A6 is checked by `scripts/check-workspace-deps.sh`: the normal dependency tree of the foundation-only crate holds no workspace crate other than the five. Sizes for A6: 10^6 elements for every primitive and every one of the four scalar types; radix sort on u32 keys of one word with a u32 payload. A7: ghost layouts from three seeds on each of 1–4 ranks. On a device without f64, `forward` runs in f64 (it moves u32 words) and `reverse` is expected to return an error; the test does not name the variant.
- **A8** runs the check function of A7 with `Mpi` as the communicator. A target with `harness = false` cannot be ignored, so it runs from T1 on.
- **A11** has no test of its own. The gate runs the milestone tier and compares nothing with earlier numbers: "unchanged" means that no test, tolerance or check of the standing matrix was changed to make it pass, which the gate's record pass reads from the merged diffs.

## Tasks

The check commands of `AGENTS.md` stay as they are. The scripts and workflows behind them gain what ADR-0074 ("Changes if accepted", Interfaces and tests; Process documents and scripts) asks for. The sections of T0–T3, T5 and T6 name the files and give those tasks that job (rule 3).

T1 needs the answers to questions 1 and 2, and T2 the answer to question 3, with the ADR that records them.

**Stubs.** A move has no interface to stub, so T0 and T1 use three kinds:

- *Error enums* are declared whole and `#[doc(hidden)]` (variants cannot be stubbed), without `Display` and `std::error::Error`. Their `From` conversions into `GridError` and tetraloma's helper have the body `unimplemented!("pending M18.T<k>")`.
- *`FieldScalar`* is declared hidden with its four implementations and `PARTS = 0`, the stand-in for `unimplemented!` in a constant.
- *The two test-support crates* start as shims that re-export from tetraloma-testing (T1). T6 turns the dependency round. The tests of A6–A8 are final from T1 on and pass through the shims; what fails until T6 is the dependency clause of A6.

**Regression guard.** A6 and A7 carry `#[ignore = "pending M18.T6"]` until T6. Because they exercise the paths that T2–T5 move, T1 adds a step to the `cpu` job that runs them with `--ignored`, and T2–T5 run them locally. T6 removes the markers and the step.

**Order.** Each of T2–T5 leaves the workspace building and every existing test passing. `GridError` stays in tetraloma-device until T5, with temporary `From` conversions next to it, so the mesh crates keep compiling while the foundation crates change their error types one at a time.

## Open questions

1. **The foundation-only test crate has no name and no row in section 18.** ADR-0074 calls it "the foundation-only test crate of M18" and lists it among the new unpublished crates, but the crate table was not given a row. A new row is a design edit, so it needs a short ADR, as ADR-0072 was for tetraloma-examples. Recommended: the name `tetraloma-foundation-tests`, unpublished, with the row "The checks and tests of the foundation crates without a mesh crate: primitives at 10^6 elements and the scatterer on thread ranks; forwards runtime features | tetraloma-device, tetraloma-parallel, tetraloma-comm-testing, tetraloma-testing-base; mpi (optional)". The name follows tetraloma-mpi-tests and uses the design's word for the four crates.
2. **Where the MPI run of A8 lives.** Section 22 says MPI tests live in tetraloma-mpi-tests, which depends on tetraloma. Two ways:
   - a. A target `foundation_scatter` in tetraloma-mpi-tests that calls the check function of the foundation-only crate. `scripts/mpi-test.sh` and section 22 stay as they are; the row of tetraloma-mpi-tests in section 18 gains the dependency (the same ADR as question 1). The check that runs is the same, but the MPI executable is built with the facade in its dependency tree.
   - b. An MPI target inside the foundation-only crate. Then `scripts/mpi-test.sh` (a protected path) must build and run a second package, the MPI harness (`run`, the abort hook, the rank guard's call) needs a home below the mesh, and the MPI-tests rule of section 22 changes.

   Recommended: a. It costs one dependency line and leaves a protected path alone. Option b is what the first crate without a mesh will need for MPI tests of its own, and it can bring it then.
3. **Does `tetraloma` re-export the four new error types?** Section 18 lists no such re-export (item 16 above). Without one, code that implements `Partitioner` or `CommDyn` through `tetraloma` cannot name the error it must return: the book's `RoundRobin` in tetraloma-examples, and `ThreadComm` and the test partitioners of tetraloma-testing until T6. Two ways:
   - a. `tetraloma` re-exports `CommError`, `DeviceError`, `ParallelError` and `PartitionError` at its root, not in the prelude (the same ADR: the row of tetraloma in section 18, and the paragraph after Listing S1).
   - b. No re-export. tetraloma-examples gains the dependencies tetraloma-comm and tetraloma-partition (its row in section 18), tetraloma-testing gains tetraloma-comm, and the book tells a user who writes a partitioner to depend on tetraloma-partition.

   Recommended: a. A user of the facade then needs no second crate to write a partitioner or a communicator, which is what the facade is for.
